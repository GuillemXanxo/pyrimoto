## Who I am and how Pyrimoto began

I'm Guillem, a lover of motorcycles, travel and adventure.

For years, I've found myself planning motorcycle trips and getaways for friends or simply for myself. And there was always something that came up when we got back: people would tell me how surprised they were by the roads, the landscapes, the restaurants or the stories behind the places we'd passed through.

That's where Pyrimoto began.

From the desire to share this way of travelling and experiencing motorcycles with more people: with you.

Because it's not just about finding a great road. It's about discovering what's waiting at the end of it, what we find along the way and what we bring home when the journey is over.

## Motorcycles have always been part of my life

There have always been motorcycles in my family.

When I was three, my father used to take me around Barcelona on a Honda CB250. It's one of the strongest memories I have from those early years.

Later came a Suzuki GS500, a Yamaha Thundercat and a BMW R100R Roadster.

So, naturally, I couldn't wait to turn fourteen and get a moped of my own. I was counting the days.

As soon as I could, I got my licence, and ever since, there has always been a motorcycle in my garage.

These days, that's a 1995 BMW K75S “Ultima” and a 2008 Honda CBF600SA. Who knows what will be in the garage by the time we meet...

## Motorcycles that ask you to be present

Although there has always been a motorcycle in my garage, I haven't always looked for the same experience from them. And that means I haven't always ridden the same kind of motorcycle.

Over the years, I've learned to appreciate everything that doesn't show up on the speedometer. To enjoy motorcycles that reward you for being present, rather than for pushing to the limit.

They tend to be bikes with very little between your right hand and the rear wheel.

Mechanical, direct motorcycles that let you feel what's happening underneath you — and that ask for something in return: your attention and your patience.

## What riding means to me

For me, riding a motorcycle means finishing a journey with a huge smile on my face.

A day spent on a motorcycle feels different from a day without one. It's difficult to explain exactly why, but that's part of the magic of these machines.

It's not just about movement. It's the feeling of having truly been there, paying attention to every kilometre.

## The road is about much more than speed

If speed were all that mattered, we'd be better off riding on a racetrack.

We ride on the open road to enjoy everything beyond the act of riding itself: the shadows cast by trees, the way changing light transforms a landscape, an unfamiliar road or a corner with a little gravel or dampness that makes us slow down and pay even more attention.

And then there are the other inhabitants of the road: tractors, cars, cyclists and all the unexpected things that make a road feel real.

The road isn't a racetrack. And that's part of the journey too.

## Designing a route is only the beginning

My routes usually begin with a map.

I look for the most interesting way to get from one place that catches my attention to another. A road that takes a strange detour for no obvious reason. A mountain pass I've never ridden. A stretch of road that makes you look at the map and think: “I need to go through there.”

Then reality always has something to add.

There might be snow, a closed road or some other unexpected obstacle. And there are days when I'm simply tired and would rather get to the next place quickly.

Because designing a route is one thing. Living it is another.

And a good route needs enough room to change along the way.

## A little bit of adventure

I'd like Pyrimoto to make you take that road you've always wondered about — wondering where it goes or what you'll find there — but never took because you never gave yourself the time.

To reconnect you with that sense of adventure and curiosity that we sometimes lose along the way.

To give you permission to get it wrong, arrive somewhere you didn't expect and have to turn around.

Because that detour might just be the thing you remember most.

**That's Pyrimoto.**

An excuse to get on your motorcycle, waste a little time and discover what's waiting beyond the road you already know.
